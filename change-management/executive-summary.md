## 1. Purpose
This document gives executive sponsors and business leadership a one-page view of the people and process change required to deliver INIT-001, without needing to read the six epic-level and twenty-one feature-level change briefs individually.

## 2. The Change in One Paragraph
New group onboarding moves from a fragmented, manual process — email-based intake, unstructured underwriting submissions, document exchange by email, informal work handoffs, ad hoc broker communication, and manually compiled status reporting — to a guided, digital, and measurable experience for plan sponsors, brokers, underwriters, and internal operations teams. The technology change is the easy part; the harder part is retiring the manual habits, channels, and informal tracking that this initiative is designed to replace.

## 3. Why This Matters
- **Problem today:** manual exchanges, fragmented information, delayed validation, and limited status visibility slow onboarding and create rework.
- **Cost of inaction:** these issues persist, sponsor experience remains inconsistent, and internal teams continue absorbing the cost of chasing missing or incorrect information.
- **Value expected:** faster cycle time, improved first-pass completeness, less manual handling, and transparent, evidence-based reporting on results.

## 5. Who Is Affected
| Stakeholder group | Nature of impact | Impact level |
|---|---|---|
| Plan sponsor administrators | New self-service digital journey replaces manual forms, email, and spreadsheets | High |
| Brokers | New portal access and structured collaboration replace informal email/phone exchanges | High |
| Underwriters | New structured intake and referral workflow replace free-form review and informal tracking | High |
| New business administrators | Shift from data entry/chasing to exception handling and review | High |
| Operations managers | Shift from reactive, manual status-chasing to proactive, dashboard-driven management | High |
| Business leaders / Product Manager | Shift from ad hoc reporting to a standing, evidence-based KPI scorecard | Medium |

## 6. Top Cross-Cutting Risks
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Users revert to familiar manual channels (email, spreadsheets, phone) after go-live | Set explicit cutover dates and restrict/retire legacy channels epic by epic |
| Business rules and KPI baselines/targets remain unapproved at launch | Track decisions and approvals through each epic's readiness criteria before go-live |
| Adoption stalls without visible leadership sponsorship | Executive sponsor visibly backs the communication and training plan for each epic |
| Benefits are hard to demonstrate without a trusted data source | Prioritize EPIC-006 dashboard/KPI rollout early enough to establish baselines |

## 7. Readiness Status
Each epic change brief includes its own go-live checklist (stakeholder briefing, communications, training, hypercare support, fallback plan). Leadership's role is to confirm, epic by epic:
- [ ] Change sponsor named for the epic
- [ ] Communication and training plans approved
- [ ] Adoption measures and owners agreed
- [ ] Go-live and hypercare support confirmed

## 9. Decisions and Support Needed From Leadership
- Approve KPI baselines and targets before each epic's go-live (see source epics' KPI sections).
- Name a change sponsor and communication owner per epic.
- Confirm legacy-channel retirement dates (email intake, manual document exchange, informal escalation) so adoption isn't undermined by parallel processes.
- Confirm hypercare support model and fallback plan for the first cycle of each epic.
